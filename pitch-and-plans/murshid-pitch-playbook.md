# Murshid: Winning Playbook for the Create AI Agents Championship (Youth)
Prepared 10 Oct 2026

## 0. What wins here (evidence)
- **Judges reward real, personal problems.** Builder.ai's founder, who mentored the 2024 cohort: "the winners have all solved very real problems… showed unique insight." Emudy (2024 Youth winner, then App of the Year) came from the founders' own EmSAT pain and was pitched with a sized market of "an estimated 110,000 students every year." [KT]
- **The final pitch is short and public-facing.** In 2024 the finalists had **90 seconds**, and the audience voted Emudy App of the Year. [KT] Your one-line hook has to land in that time.
- **A narrow wedge with a concrete action wins.** BieBite (2025 Youth) does one thing: "scan → allergen check → buy." The founder told her story as a student, and the site shows a traction number ("532 beta users"). [MDX blog, biebite.com] Ettan Ehsan (2026 Youth) is "school subjects → short gamified challenges." POP by Kwelio (2026 App of the Year) pitched behavioural science and predictive analytics, plus a **B2B2C payer (insurers)**. [Entrepreneur ME, kwelio.com]
- **Education is a crowded category** (second-largest by entries in 2025, after health), so you need sharper differentiation. [Dubai Media Office]
- **What this championship asks for:** an agent that "reasons, plans, uses tools and takes action" (more than a chatbot). You must prove customer need, business potential and measurable impact. Finalists show a **fully functional agent live, not an MVP**. The rules also say "the solution must protect personal… data and prevent information leakage." There are 24 semi-finalists and 12 finalists, and each category winner gets **USD 150,000**. [Programme page]
- **Market size:** 406,563 students in Dubai private schools in 2026–27, across 227+ schools. [Gulf Education/KHDA, KHDA]

**Strategic call:** keep the big "manager for every student" vision on one slide. For the January demo, lead with one wedge: **"the weekly briefing + opportunity finder that turns school notices and deadlines into a plan."** Judges back a sharp wedge with a believable roadmap over a do-everything agent.

---

## 1. Pitch deck (13 slides)
1. **Title/hook:** "Every athlete has a manager. Now every student does." Logo, your name, "Grade 11, GEMS Our Own, Dubai."
2. **The problem, from you:** a photo of your real week: portal notices, WhatsApp groups, missed deadlines. *Collect:* how many notices or messages you get per week (count them over 2 weeks) and one opportunity you missed.
3. **The problem in numbers:** survey results (e.g., "X% missed a deadline or opportunity this term", "avg Y hrs/week organising"). *Collect:* n ≥ 100 survey responses and 15 interviews.
4. **Who it hurts:** the student, the parent (no visibility) and the school (low engagement with notices). Use 2 interview quotes, one each from a student and a parent.
5. **Solution:** Murshid in one sentence and 3 icons: *Knows you → Plans for you → Acts for you.*
6. **How the agent works:** diagram: Inputs (portal notes, calendar, goals) → Planner (multi-model router) → Tools (calendar, opportunity database, CV builder) → Actions (briefing, reminders, application drafts). Show the human-approval checkpoint.
7. **Demo screenshots:** the Sunday briefing, an opportunity match, and a drafted application.
8. **Proof it works (pilot):** *Collect:* number of pilot students, weekly active %, briefings opened, deadlines met before vs after, opportunities applied to, and NPS. Include one teacher or counsellor quote.
9. **Why now / why us:** UAE AI strategy, the 406k-student market [KHDA], and the fact that you are the user.
10. **Business model:** Parents pay AED 29–49/month (test this price in the survey). Schools pay a per-student annual licence (e.g., AED 60–100). Universities or programmes could later pay for verified lead placement (opt-in). *Collect:* parent willingness to pay, and 1–2 school letters of interest.
11. **Market and scale:** TAM is UAE K-12 grades 8–12, SAM is Dubai private grades 9–12, and the SOM is 10 schools in year 1. Expansion path: GCC, then curriculum-agnostic.
12. **Competition:** a 2×2 chart, *generic ↔ knows the student* against *answers ↔ acts*. ChatGPT, school LMS, counsellor platforms and Murshid sit in the four quadrants.
13. **Trust, roadmap and ask:** privacy by design (consent, parent dashboard, no essay ghost-writing). Milestones run Jan to May, and the USD 150k goes to engineering, security and the school pilot rollout.

## 2. Demo video script (2:30)
- **0:00–0:15:** cold open. Phone buzzing with 14 school notices. VO: "This was my Sunday."
- **0:15–0:30:** hook line and logo. "Murshid: a personal agent that manages a student's life."
- **0:30–0:55:** onboarding. The student states goals (e.g., "engineering, UAE or UK, robotics") and Murshid builds an identity profile.
- **0:55–1:25:** **agent at work** (screen record). Murshid ingests portal notes, extracts 3 deadlines, books study blocks into Google Calendar and flags a clash. Show the reasoning trace ("planning → tool call → result") so judges see it is an agent.
- **1:25–1:50:** opportunity finder. It matches a real UAE competition or summer programme, explains why it fits, drafts the application and **waits for the student's approval**.
- **1:50–2:05:** the weekly briefing for the student and a summary for the parent (no private chats shared).
- **2:05–2:20:** pilot numbers on screen and a 5-second student testimonial.
- **2:20–2:30:** vision and close. "Built in Dubai, for every student."

## 3. Validation plan (Oct 2026 – Jan 2027)
- **Interviews (Oct–Nov):** 10 students across grades 9–12 and different curricula, 5 parents, 3 teachers or counsellors, and 1 school leader. Ask about the last time they missed something, how they track work today, and what they would pay for or forbid.
- **Survey (Google Form, target n ≥ 100):**
  1. Grade and curriculum
  2. Where you get school notices (portal, WhatsApp, email, other)
  3. In the last month, how many deadlines or tasks did you miss or nearly miss? (0 / 1–2 / 3+)
  4. Hours per week spent organising rather than studying
  5. Have you missed a competition, internship or scholarship because you found out too late? (Y/N)
  6. How confident are you about your university or career plan? (1–5)
  7. Would you let an AI read your school portal if you approve every action? (Y / Maybe / N)
  8. Most wanted feature (rank: planner, opportunities, CV, briefing, study plan)
  9. Parents only: monthly price you would consider fair (AED 0 / 19 / 39 / 59+)
  10. Email for beta access (opt-in)
- **Pilot at school (Dec–Jan):** 20–30 students for 4 weeks. Get counsellor or teacher sponsorship and parental consent forms. Students forward or paste portal notes themselves at first, so you need no system integration. Start with a 1-week baseline (no Murshid), then 3 weeks with it.
- **Metrics:** weekly active rate, briefings opened, tasks completed on time (self-reported against baseline), opportunities surfaced and applied to, time saved (self-reported), NPS, retention in week 3, and the % of parents who say they would pay.

## 4. Week-by-week plan (~12–15 hrs/week)
**Phase A: Discover (12 Oct – 8 Nov)**
- W1 (12 Oct): Confirm Youth registration on the portal. Write interview guides and launch the survey.
- W2 (19 Oct): Run 8 interviews and attend the Agentic AI workshop.
- W3 (26 Oct): Finish interviews, push the survey to 100+ responses and analyse it. Lock the wedge.
- W4 (2 Nov): Pick the stack (e.g., Next.js/Expo, Supabase, an LLM router, Google Calendar API). Wireframes. Approach the counsellor about a pilot.

**Phase B: Build the MVP (9 Nov – 13 Dec)**
- W5: Auth, onboarding and the goal profile.
- W6: Note ingestion (paste or upload) → deadline extraction → calendar actions.
- W7: Opportunity database (seed 50 UAE opportunities by hand) and matching.
- W8: Weekly briefing, plus the approval and consent layer.
- W9: Internal test with 5 friends, fix bugs, privacy policy. Get pilot consent forms signed.

**Phase C: Pilot and submit (14 Dec – mid-Jan)**
- W10–11 (term break): Run the baseline week, then launch the pilot. Draft the deck.
- W12: Collect metrics mid-pilot and record the demo footage.
- W13: Edit the video, finalise the deck with real numbers, and do a dry run with a teacher.
- W14: **Submit early**: prototype link, deck and video. The exact January date is not yet published, so check the WhatsApp channel.

**Phase D: Semi-final (late Jan – March)**
- Join the accelerator if selected. Add CV/portfolio and an application-draft agent. Expand the pilot to a second grade or school. Pitch in front of family and teachers 10 times, with 3-minute and 90-second versions.

**Phase E: Final (April – first week of May)**
- Harden for a live demo: an offline fallback recording, a seeded demo account and rate-limit buffers. Run a security review. Get a school letter of intent. Over the last 2 weeks, run daily rehearsals, then freeze the code 5 days before the final.

## 5. Risks and judge Q&A
- **Privacy (minors' data):** "Consent-first: parent and student consent, data minimisation, encryption, no training on student data, and a delete-anything button. Every outbound action needs student approval. Aligned with UAE PDPL; school data access only via official partnership." (This also meets the championship's data-protection rule.)
- **Cheating:** "Murshid plans, finds and coaches; it does not write assessed work. Essay help is limited to feedback and outlines, and teachers can view a usage log."
- **Why not ChatGPT?** "ChatGPT waits for a prompt. Murshid already knows your timetable, goals and deadlines, then acts on them every week without being asked. It also has a curated UAE opportunity database, and the multi-model router keeps cost per student low."
- **School adoption:** "We start B2C with parents, so we don't depend on IT approvals. The pilot gives us data for schools, and schools buy because it raises engagement and improves counselling outcomes."
- **Monetisation:** "Parent subscription plus school licences. Survey: X% of parents would pay ≥ AED 39."
- **Execution risk (solo 17-year-old):** "Narrow MVP, open-source stack, accelerator mentorship. The grant funds a contractor for security and mobile."
- **Hallucinated opportunities:** "Every opportunity comes from a verified database with a source link. The model ranks; it doesn't invent."
